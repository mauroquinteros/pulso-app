# A Quote is read apart from the History, and its absence has three causes

[ADR 0008](0008-prices-arrive-by-cron-not-by-request.md) settled how a price *arrives* — a cron writes it into `stocks` and the client only ever selects. This settles how the client *consumes* it. The app reads `stocks` **separately from the History and in parallel with it**, filtered to `price is not null`, whenever the tabs mount or the app returns from the background. The read **never gates the app**: a **Perfil** with no **Quote** for anything still sees their **Movements**, their **Efectivo** and their **History**, because a **Stock** is shared and sits outside the all-or-nothing rule — a missing Stock costs a **Market Value**, not a wrong one. What a Quote's absence *means* is therefore not one thing but three, and the app must say which.

## Considered Options

- **Fold `stocks` into the all-or-nothing read.** Four selects, one verdict, one error screen — the machinery already exists in `lib/history.ts` and would need almost nothing added. Rejected because it inverts the rule it would be reusing. All-or-nothing exists because a **History** missing some Movements is *wrong*, not smaller; a portfolio missing some prices is genuinely smaller, and `valueHolding` already reports it honestly. Adopting it here would mean one unquotable ticker — a state `refresh-stocks` reaches by *skipping*, on purpose — blocking a user from reading their own movements.
- **Read only the tickers the Perfil holds.** Downloads exactly what is used and stays bounded by the user's own portfolio rather than by how many people use the app. Rejected because held tickers are derived from the History, so the price read would have to *wait* for it. The two reads become serial, a failed History means no prices at all, and the price read can no longer fire on foreground without re-deriving its own `in` list. The read is unfiltered instead, which the schema already blesses: `stocks` has no `user_id` and its RLS policy is `using (true)`.
- **One flat missing-price policy — exclude and flag, whatever the reason.** Today's behaviour, and zero new concepts. Rejected on the evidence of what it prints: with no prices at all, `assemblePortfolio` yields `marketValue = 0`, so **Inicio** shows *Aportado $5,000 → Vale hoy $50* and a **Total Return** near -100%, with the only caveat on a different tab. A dropped packet and a wiped-out portfolio must not be the same screen.
- **Put the read's status inside the engine.** `assemblePortfolio` would take the status and every price-applied figure would come out already knowing why it is absent, so no screen could get the sentence wrong. Rejected: it drags an asynchronous lifecycle into a pure function that derives figures from facts, and every engine test grows an argument it does not care about. The **History** already demonstrates the alternative — its status lives in the store, never inside `Movement[]`.
- **Type the Quote as nullable, mirroring the schema.** `price` is genuinely nullable and `resolve-stock` genuinely writes rows without one, so this is the literal truth. Rejected in favour of moving the guarantee into the query: `where price is not null` makes `quote` non-optional in the domain, and an unpriced Stock simply does not appear — falling through the `price === undefined` path `valueHolding` has always had. The type states something true without a branch existing to enforce it.

## Consequences

- **Three causes, three sentences.** A Quote **not yet read**, a Quote that **could not be obtained**, and a **Stock that has no Quote** because the provider has none. Only the third is a fact about the Stock, and only the third is permanent. They render alike — a **Holding** with no Market Value — which is exactly why they are named apart, as `CONTEXT.md` already names the three faces of a missing History.
- **A failed *refresh* is a fourth thing, and not an absence at all.** When a re-read fails, the Quotes already in hand stay on screen and a banner says the prices could not be updated. Hiding them would report a fault as an absence; saying nothing would present a Quote of unknown age as current. Note this needs **no market calendar** — announcing that an attempt failed is a fact about the app, whereas **Stale Price** is a judgement about a Quote the app has, measured against market activity. The Stale signal is deliberately *not* lit by this decision.
- **A `quoted_at` is carried even though nothing reads it yet.** The client keeps the whole Quote — price and the market moment it belongs to — because the schema's `price_and_quote_time_travel_together` constraint exists precisely so the pair is never split, and lighting the Stale signal later must be a pure function over data already in hand rather than a re-read.
- **Inicio flags a partial miss and refuses a total one.** Excluding some holdings is a caveat on a number and stays the documented policy — `CONTEXT.md`'s **Allocation** entry defines **Total Portfolio Value** as the sum over priced holdings. Excluding *all* of them leaves no number to caveat, so Vale hoy and Total Return decline to print. **Efectivo** stays on screen throughout, because **Cash** derives from the History alone and is still exactly right: the app can honestly say what is held in cash while refusing to say what it is all worth.
- **The Quote store is emptied on sign-out — for freshness, not privacy.** A Quote is shared, so nothing leaks; `clearPerfilScopedState` therefore stops meaning "clear the stores" and starts meaning "clear the Perfil-scoped stores", which has to be said in the comment or the exemption rots. The reason it is cleared anyway: Quotes surviving into a session whose own read *fails* would be valued as current with no way to say otherwise, collapsing the three causes into two at the moment the third one matters.
- **The trigger is the tabs mounting, not the app launching.** `Stack.Protected` removes `(tabs)` from the tree when the session guard falls, so mount is the one event that covers a cold start and a fresh sign-in identically, while tab switches and pushing a detail screen do not fire it. This is deliberately unlike `RequireHistory`, which triggers on `status === "unread"` and never on mount: a History is re-read only when it is genuinely gone, whereas every new session should ask for Quotes again, because ten minutes may have passed or ten hours.
- **A real Stock can be invisible to the app.** Filtering on `price is not null` means an unpriced Stock is absent from the map, so its `name` is unavailable too — a holding with no price could not show "Apple Inc." beside its ticker. Nothing shows the name today. The day something does, that is the cost.
- **`resolve-stock` retries its quote once, and still writes the Stock when that fails.** The nullable `price` is not a consequence of needing two Finnhub calls — that much is forced, since no free endpoint carries name, price and market moment together (`/quote` has no name, `/search` no price, `/stock/profile2` answers `{}` for ETFs, `/etf/profile` is premium). It is a consequence of writing the row when the *second* call fails, which is deliberate: ADR 0009's confirmation is the exact symbol match, and the price is not part of the verdict. What the retry removes is the common accident — a transient blip minting an unpriced Stock, leaving a brand-new holding reading "sin precio" until the next cron pass, which over a weekend is Monday. One retry with a 250ms pause, the same shape `lib/history.ts` uses against `PGRST303`, and for the same reason: the fault clears in moments or it is not this fault.
- **Considered and deferred, so the reasoning is not re-derived.** *Checking `stocks` before calling Finnhub* — a row exists only because Finnhub already confirmed it, so the table is ADR 0009's own accumulated output and reading it first would cost zero external calls on the common path. Deferred because it makes a **Stock**'s `name` write-once, and because a delisted symbol would then keep confirming forever, letting a user record a new buy of a company that no longer trades. *A delisting detector* — `refresh-stocks` already asks about every ticker every ten minutes, and a `200` with `t: 0` is Finnhub stating it does not know the symbol rather than a transport failure, so the cron is a detector that currently discards its evidence; a skipped row keeps its last good price forever, which is a wrong **Market Value** with nothing on screen saying so. Deferred as its own slice: it needs a column, a migration, a split of unquotable from unreachable, and a rule that only a successful re-validation clears the flag — because a reassigned ticker starts quoting again, and a flag the cron could clear would silently clear itself. *Making `price` NOT NULL* — reachable only by refusing to write on a failed quote, which would block a buy of a Stock the app can already price. It is also **mechanically incompatible** with writing the Stock anyway, which is worth knowing before anyone proposes it again: PostgREST builds its column list from the JSON payload's keys, so a `{ticker, name}` upsert proposes an insert tuple holding `price = NULL`, and Postgres checks constraints on that tuple **before** `ON CONFLICT` resolves to an update. A NOT NULL `price` would therefore refuse the write even for a Stock already sitting in the table with a good price ([PostgREST #4046](https://github.com/PostgREST/postgrest/issues/4046), and [postgrest-js #515](https://github.com/supabase/postgrest-js/issues/515) closed wontfix). The omitted-column *preservation* the write path relies on is real — `price` and `quoted_at` are absent from the generated `DO UPDATE SET`, so an existing price survives — but it holds only because both columns are nullable and are always omitted **together**, which is what keeps `price_and_quote_time_travel_together` satisfied on the proposed tuple.
- **`MOCK_PRICES` retires from production and stays as test data.** Its two callers — `hooks/use-portfolio.ts` and `app/stock/[ticker].tsx` — are the last mock data the running app touches. `MOCK_MOVEMENTS` remains as the reconciliation corpus: thirteen movements across all five types, one partial sell and one full exit, which is what makes the Cash Impact, Inicio and Allocation invariants provable at all.
